# The Atlas of Remainders

## Book II of *How Sentences Come to Rest*

### A Field Guide to the 54 Forms of Completion

**Formal spine:** Operator · Orientation · Terminal Mode → Invariant

An invariant is the stable remainder produced when a field of possible completion contracts through exactly one operator, one orientation, and one terminal mode.

This book is not a taxonomy of meanings. It is a field guide to landings: the finite ways an intelligible act comes to rest.

Expression opens. Contraction narrows. Completion lands. What lands is not a theme, mood, motive, or interpretation. It is a remainder.

---

# Preface — The Second Book

The first book, *How Sentences Come to Rest*, establishes the central claim: an intelligible act is complete only when it lands. This second book asks what kinds of landings are possible.

A sentence does not finish merely because it ends typographically. It finishes when the open field of continuations, relations, implications, actions, and structural possibilities has contracted into a stable remainder. That remainder is the invariant.

The invariant is not selected by resemblance. It is cultivated from a seed and then fixed by exclusion. At the beginning of analysis, a seed pressure appears: a cut, an opening, a dependence, a correction, a unity, or an ending demand. That seed is not yet the invariant. It must be cultivated through three determinations.

First, the terminal mode must collapse. The landing must leave either state, held plurality, or enacted result. These are named Is, With, and Does.

Second, the orientation must collapse. Attention must gather, span, or release. These are named Toward, Across, and Away.

Third, the operator must collapse by dominance. The dominant operator is the transformation without which the act would not land in the same way.

Only after these three determinations survive rereading can the invariant be declared.

Every chapter in this book therefore does two things. It states what the invariant is, and it defines the invariant by exactly what it is not. The negative definition is not secondary. It is the guardrail that prevents the atlas from becoming metaphor, impression, or ordinary semantic classification.

# The Chapter Method

Each invariant chapter follows this sequence.

## 1. Seed

The chapter begins with the seed pressure: the first unstable structural tendency from which the invariant may be cultivated.

## 2. Cultivation

The seed is cultivated by collapsing terminal mode, orientation, and operator dominance.

## 3. Coordinate

The coordinate is declared:

**Operator · Orientation · Terminal Mode → Invariant**

## 4. What It Is

The invariant is defined as a stable landing.

## 5. Why It Must Be

The chapter shows why the invariant follows necessarily from the coordinate.

## 6. What It Is Not

The chapter excludes neighboring and rival invariants.

## 7. Operational Use

The chapter states when to apply and when to reject the invariant.

# Part I — Distinction

Distinction produces rest by cut. Something is made to stand apart from what it is not.

## I01 — Distinction · Toward · Is → Simple Placement

The seed of Simple Placement is the first possible cut: something begins to stand apart from what it is not. At the seed stage, nothing yet determines whether the result will become a bare placement, a held alternative, an enacted boundary, an opposition, a selection, or some richer structure. The seed only says: there is enough distinguishability for one term to be taken up.

Cultivation begins by collapsing the terminal mode to Is. The landing cannot require a held plurality and cannot require an enacted operation. What remains is a state. Orientation then collapses to Toward. Attention gathers to one focal term rather than spanning a field or releasing away from one. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because the landing depends on the cut itself rather than on expansion, relation, correction, integration, or closure.

**Simple Placement** is the form of completion in which a single term stands as what it is after being cut from what it is not. Its formula is: X is Y. The landing is minimal identification.

It must be Simple Placement because Toward gathers, Is leaves a state, and Distinction supplies the cut. No plurality remains. No action remains. No range, release, dependence, adjustment, unity, or finality is required.

It is not Twofold Division, because Twofold Division preserves a split. It is not Boundary Imposition, because no boundary remains as an enacted result. It is not Direct Pairing, because no second term is structurally required. It is not Established Fact, because the act has not ended all further demand; it has only placed the term.

Use Simple Placement when an act comes to rest by fixing one term as one identity. Reject it whenever alternatives, relations, actions, scope, opposition, or closure are required.

## I02 — Distinction · Toward · With → Twofold Division

The seed of Twofold Division is a cut that does not collapse into one term. Something is distinguished, but the distinction leaves a bounded alternative structure. The field begins to divide.

Cultivation first collapses terminal mode to With. More than one possibility remains structurally present. Orientation collapses to Toward, because the plurality still converges toward a focal placement within a division. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because the landing is produced by cut rather than relation or integration.

**Twofold Division** is the form of completion in which a bounded plurality converges into one placement while retaining a split. Its formula is: X is one of Y1/Y2.

It must be Twofold Division because With requires retained plurality, Toward requires convergence, and Distinction requires a cut. The result is neither one bare identity nor an enacted separation, but a held alternative.

It is not Simple Placement, because more than one possibility remains. It is not Boundary Imposition, because the division is held rather than enacted. It is not Polar Opposition, because the alternatives have not been released into opposed standing. It is not Gradual Differentiation, because the alternatives are bounded rather than continuous.

Use Twofold Division when completion requires a split whose alternatives remain present at rest. Reject it when only one term remains, when the boundary itself acts, or when the split becomes opposition.

## I03 — Distinction · Toward · Does → Boundary Imposition

The seed of Boundary Imposition is a cut becoming operative. A distinction does not merely allow recognition; it begins to do separating work.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. The landing requires an enacted result. Orientation collapses to Toward, because the action gathers into a focal boundary. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because separation is what produces rest.

**Boundary Imposition** is the form of completion in which a cut is actively imposed as the result. Its formula is: X is separated from Y by boundary B.

It must be Boundary Imposition because Does requires enactment, Toward requires focal convergence, and Distinction supplies the separating operation. The boundary is not merely noticed; it is imposed.

It is not Simple Placement, because the result is not bare identity. It is not Twofold Division, because the alternatives are not merely held. It is not Local Differentiation, because the cut is focal rather than scoped inside a retained span. It is not Singular Selection, because alternatives are separated rather than eliminated. It is not Limit Recalibration, because the operator is cut, not adjustment.

Use Boundary Imposition when the act lands by drawing or enforcing a boundary. Reject it when the act merely identifies, classifies, selects, localizes, or adjusts a boundary already in play.

## I04 — Distinction · Across · Is → Identity Constriction

The seed of Identity Constriction is a cut that narrows what something may be. Identity appears, but under pressure from a limiting condition.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Is. The landing is a state. Orientation collapses to Across, because the limiting distinction remains active across a span. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because the identity is produced by narrowing through a cut.

**Identity Constriction** is the form of completion in which identity is limited by a retained distinction across a span. Its formula is: X is constrained to Y.

It must be Identity Constriction because Is leaves identity as state, Across retains the limiting field, and Distinction supplies the narrowing cut. The result is not bare identity but identity under constraint.

It is not Simple Placement, because identity is not bare. It is not Absence Marking, because absence is not held as a structural partner. It is not Local Differentiation, because no difference is enacted. It is not Limit Recalibration, because the boundary has not been reset.

Use Identity Constriction when an act lands by stating what something is only under an active constraint. Reject it when the constraint becomes absence, local action, adjustment, or mere placement.

## I05 — Distinction · Across · With → Absence Marking

The seed of Absence Marking is presence carrying a structural lack. Something appears, but it appears together with what is not there.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. Presence and absence must remain together. Orientation collapses to Across, because the present-absent distinction spans a retained field. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because the landing depends on the cut between presence and absence.

**Absence Marking** is the form of completion in which presence is defined against absence across a retained distinction. Its formula is: X marks the absence of Y.

It must be Absence Marking because With requires co-presence, Across retains the contrast, and Distinction supplies the presence-absence cut. The absence does not vanish. It remains structurally active.

It is not Identity Constriction, because the landing is relational rather than merely stative. It is not Local Differentiation, because difference is not enacted. It is not Polar Opposition, because absence is marked rather than opposed. It is not Complete Cessation, because nothing necessarily ends.

Use Absence Marking when completion requires absence to remain active as absence. Reject it when the act merely narrows identity, creates local difference, opposes terms, or terminates activity.

## I06 — Distinction · Across · Does → Local Differentiation

The seed of Local Differentiation is a cut becoming active inside a limited scope. Difference appears, but the wider field remains intact.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. The difference must be enacted. Orientation collapses to Across, because the action occurs inside a retained span. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because the landing is produced by difference-making.

**Local Differentiation** is the form of completion in which difference is enacted within a limited scope. Its formula is: X differs from Y only within S.

It must be Local Differentiation because Does requires action, Across retains scope, and Distinction supplies the cut. The result is real difference, but local.

It is not Boundary Imposition, because the cut is scoped rather than focal. It is not Absence Marking, because the remainder is enacted rather than held. It is not Singular Selection, because alternatives are not eliminated. It is not Gradual Differentiation, because the difference is scoped rather than released as continuum.

Use Local Differentiation when an act produces difference only inside a retained scope. Reject it when the distinction is global, absent, eliminative, or continuous.

## I07 — Distinction · Away · Is → Polar Opposition

The seed of Polar Opposition is a cut released into opposed standing. Difference no longer remains a bounded division or local contrast; the terms stand against one another.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Is. The landing is a state. Orientation collapses to Away, because the common field falls away. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because opposition is produced by cut.

**Polar Opposition** is the form of completion in which identity stands in opposition after release. Its formula is: X stands in opposition to Y.

It must be Polar Opposition because Is leaves standing, Away releases the shared field, and Distinction supplies the separating cut. The result is opposed identity.

It is not Twofold Division, because bounded alternatives are not held. It is not Gradual Differentiation, because the difference is polar rather than continuous. It is not Singular Selection, because no exclusionary act selects one term. It is not Oppositional Test, because the opposition is not being used correctively.

Use Polar Opposition when a term comes to rest by standing against another. Reject it when the split is held, graded, enacted as selection, or used as a corrective test.

## I08 — Distinction · Away · With → Gradual Differentiation

The seed of Gradual Differentiation is difference released from hard boundary. A cut softens into degree without disappearing.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. Plurality remains. Orientation collapses to Away, because the field releases from bounded division. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because what persists is difference, not opening, dependence, correction, unity, or closure.

**Gradual Differentiation** is the form of completion in which difference persists as a continuous gradient after release. Its formula is: X and Y differ along a continuum.

It must be Gradual Differentiation because With retains plurality, Away releases hard containment, and Distinction supplies difference. The landing is degree.

It is not Polar Opposition, because opposition is discrete. It is not Singular Selection, because no alternative is eliminated. It is not Continuous Diffusion, because the operator is difference rather than expansion. It is not Formal Correspondence, because no pattern relation dominates.

Use Gradual Differentiation when completion preserves difference as degree. Reject it when the landing is binary, eliminative, transitional by expansion, or relational by pattern.

## I09 — Distinction · Away · Does → Singular Selection

The seed of Singular Selection is a field of alternatives beginning to collapse by exclusion. One term is not merely identified; it is made to remain while others fall away.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. Selection must happen. Orientation collapses to Away, because alternatives are released or excluded. Operator dominance collapses to Distinction, because the result is produced by cutting one from many.

**Singular Selection** is the form of completion in which one term is enacted by excluding all others. Its formula is: X is selected as the sole Y.

It must be Singular Selection because Does requires enactment, Away requires release, and Distinction supplies the separating choice. The result is exclusive selection.

It is not Boundary Imposition, because selection removes alternatives rather than merely drawing a boundary. It is not Polar Opposition, because the result is enacted rather than stative. It is not Gradual Differentiation, because no continuum remains. It is not Complete Cessation, because alternatives end only relative to selection, not absolutely.

Use Singular Selection when an act lands by choosing one term and excluding the rest. Reject it when alternatives remain held, opposed, graded, or when continuation ends absolutely.

# Part II — Expansion

Expansion produces rest by lawful opening. Something extends, branches, distributes, or increases degrees of freedom without losing structural intelligibility.

## I10 — Expansion · Toward · Is → Outward Expansion

The seed of Outward Expansion is the first pressure of a term exceeding its prior boundary. Something begins to open beyond its limit.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Is. The landing is a state, not held plurality or action. Orientation collapses to Toward, because attention gathers into one stabilized extension. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because opening is what produces rest.

**Outward Expansion** is the form of completion in which a single term stands as extended from its prior boundary. Its formula is: X expands outward into Y.

It must be Outward Expansion because Is leaves state, Toward gathers extension into one landing, and Expansion supplies lawful opening.

It is not Divergent Movement, because multiple paths do not remain. It is not Branching Proliferation, because no branches are produced. It is not Range Extension, because no retained span is dominant. It is not Edge Growth, because growth action is not the landing. It is not Identity Constriction, because the movement opens rather than narrows.

Use Outward Expansion when something opens outward while still resolving as one state. Reject it when the act preserves routes, branches, distributes, grows as action, or narrows.

## I11 — Expansion · Toward · With → Divergent Movement

The seed of Divergent Movement is one source opening into more than one direction.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. Multiple paths remain. Orientation collapses to Toward, because the source remains focal. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because opening into multiplicity produces the landing.

**Divergent Movement** is the form of completion in which multiple paths diverge from a single origin while remaining anchored to it. Its formula is: X diverges from Y along multiple paths.

It must be Divergent Movement because With holds plurality, Toward preserves source-focus, and Expansion supplies opening.

It is not Outward Expansion, because plurality remains. It is not Branching Proliferation, because production is not enacted. It is not Range Extension, because the landing is source-oriented rather than span-oriented. It is not Multiple Pathways, because the source has not been released.

Use Divergent Movement when completion requires anchored divergence. Reject it when extension is singular, branch-production occurs, the field spans as range, or source focus falls away.

## I12 — Expansion · Toward · Does → Branching Proliferation

The seed of Branching Proliferation is a source becoming productive. Opening begins to generate outcomes.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. Branches must be produced. Orientation collapses to Toward, because the branches remain source-oriented. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because lawful opening into multiplicity produces the result.

**Branching Proliferation** is the form of completion in which a source actively produces multiple outcomes. Its formula is: X branches into Y1, Y2, Y3.

It must be Branching Proliferation because Does requires enactment, Toward requires focal source, and Expansion supplies opening.

It is not Divergent Movement, because plurality is produced rather than merely held. It is not Outward Expansion, because the result is not a single state. It is not Field Distribution, because the spread is branch-like rather than field-like. It is not Generalized Production, because production is expansive rather than closure-level generality.

Use Branching Proliferation when one source actively generates multiple branches. Reject it when the result is merely extended, merely divergent, field-distributed, or generalized under closure.

## I13 — Expansion · Across · Is → Path Variation

The seed of Path Variation is extension encountering route difference. Something can reach its landing by another path.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Is. The remainder is a route condition. Orientation collapses to Across, because the routing span remains active. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because the route-space opens.

**Path Variation** is the form of completion in which extension remains open across alternative routes. Its formula is: X reaches Y via alternative path Z.

It must be Path Variation because Is leaves a state, Across retains the span, and Expansion opens possible routing.

It is not Range Extension, because plurality of reach is not the remainder. It is not Field Distribution, because no spread is enacted. It is not Multiple Pathways, because the span remains retained rather than released. It is not Sequential Linkage, because dependency is not dominant. It is not Directional Pivot, because no corrective redirection occurs.

Use Path Variation when completion depends on an alternative path rather than a new endpoint. Reject it when routes are held as range, enacted as distribution, released as future options, ordered as dependency, or redirected as correction.

## I14 — Expansion · Across · With → Range Extension

The seed of Range Extension is opening across scope, distance, or reach.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. Plural reach remains. Orientation collapses to Across, because attention retains a span. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because opening produces the range.

**Range Extension** is the form of completion in which extension holds across a range while retaining plurality. Its formula is: X extends across range Y.

It must be Range Extension because With holds plurality, Across retains range, and Expansion supplies opening.

It is not Outward Expansion, because the result is not one extended state. It is not Path Variation, because the issue is reach rather than route. It is not Field Distribution, because the spread is not enacted. It is not Divergent Movement, because the landing is not anchored to a source. It is not Formal Correspondence, because no relational pattern governs the landing.

Use Range Extension when something extends across a retained range. Reject it when there is one extension, route variation, enacted distribution, source-anchored divergence, or pattern correspondence.

## I15 — Expansion · Across · Does → Field Distribution

The seed of Field Distribution is opening becoming spread. Something begins to distribute across a field.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. Distribution must be enacted. Orientation collapses to Across, because the span remains active. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because opening produces field-like spread.

**Field Distribution** is the form of completion in which opening actively distributes across a field-like span. Its formula is: X distributes as field Y.

It must be Field Distribution because Does requires action, Across retains the field, and Expansion supplies opening.

It is not Range Extension, because distribution is enacted. It is not Path Variation, because the issue is not route condition. It is not Branching Proliferation, because the spread is field-like rather than branch-like. It is not Role Integration, because no system role is fulfilled. It is not Generalized Production, because the result is spread, not closure-level production across cases.

Use Field Distribution when an action spreads across a field. Reject it when the act merely extends, varies route, branches, integrates, or generalizes.

## I16 — Expansion · Away · Is → Multiple Pathways

The seed of Multiple Pathways is release from one route, one source, or one span. The field opens into available futures.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Is. The remainder is a state of possibility. Orientation collapses to Away, because prior containment falls away. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because opening degrees of freedom produces rest.

**Multiple Pathways** is the form of completion in which a structure permits multiple future outcomes after release. Its formula is: X permits multiple future Y options.

It must be Multiple Pathways because Is leaves possibility as state, Away releases prior constraint, and Expansion supplies opening.

It is not Divergent Movement, because no source remains focal. It is not Path Variation, because no retained span dominates. It is not Range Extension, because plurality is not held as range. It is not Branching Proliferation, because branches are not produced. It is not Established Fact, because alternatives remain open.

Use Multiple Pathways when the field comes to rest as permissive multiplicity. Reject it when paths are source-anchored, span-retained, range-held, branch-produced, or closed into fact.

## I17 — Expansion · Away · With → Continuous Diffusion

The seed of Continuous Diffusion is opening without fixed boundary. One state begins to pass into another.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. More than one state remains structurally present. Orientation collapses to Away, because fixed boundaries fall away. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because transition is produced by opening.

**Continuous Diffusion** is the form of completion in which one state transitions smoothly into another without fixed boundaries. Its formula is: X gradually transitions into Y.

It must be Continuous Diffusion because With holds plurality, Away releases fixed containment, and Expansion supplies boundaryless opening.

It is not Gradual Differentiation, because the dominant transformation is opening rather than difference. It is not Formal Correspondence, because no relational pattern is primary. It is not Directional Reversal, because no corrective reversal occurs. It is not Dynamic Equilibrium, because the plurality is not oscillating balance. It is not Retrospective Reinterpretation, because no completed state is reassigned after the fact.

Use Continuous Diffusion when completion preserves transition without discrete endpoints. Reject it when continuity is primarily difference, pattern, reversal, equilibrium, or retrospective reassignment.

## I18 — Expansion · Away · Does → Edge Growth

The seed of Edge Growth is a boundary being exceeded by expansion. Something opens past its prior limit.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. Growth must happen. Orientation collapses to Away, because the prior edge is released. Operator dominance collapses to Expansion, because opening beyond boundedness produces the result.

**Edge Growth** is the form of completion in which a structure actively grows beyond its previous limit. Its formula is: X grows at its periphery into Y.

It must be Edge Growth because Does requires enacted result, Away releases the prior boundary, and Expansion supplies opening.

It is not Outward Expansion, because the landing is enacted growth rather than state. It is not Field Distribution, because the span is released rather than retained. It is not Continuous Diffusion, because transition is not merely held. It is not Limit Recalibration, because the boundary is exceeded rather than reset. It is not Convergent Stabilization, because the result is growth rather than stable integration.

Use Edge Growth when expansion enacts growth past an edge. Reject it when the result is merely larger, distributed, transitional, recalibrated, or stabilized.
