# Part IV — Correction

Correction produces rest by adjustment. Something already in play changes standing through reweighting, redirection, reversal, recalibration, testing, transfer, or restoration.

Correction does not begin from a blank field. Something has already been placed, opened, related, or put under strain. The corrective seed appears when the existing standing is no longer adequate as it is. But correction is not yet the invariant. The seed must be cultivated through terminal mode, orientation, and operator dominance.

The Correction family is defined negatively against relation and integration. It may involve multiple terms, but dependence alone is not the point. It may stabilize a structure, but unity alone is not the point. Correction produces rest because a prior standing is modified, tested, redirected, or restored.

---

## I28 — Correction · Toward · Is → Incremental Adjustment

The seed of Incremental Adjustment is a slight pressure against an existing standing. Something does not need to be remade, reversed, restored, or transferred. It only needs to shift.

Cultivation first collapses terminal mode to Is. The landing is a corrected state, not a held balance and not an enacted transfer. Orientation collapses to Toward, because attention gathers toward a focal adjustment. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the result depends on alteration of a prior standing.

**Incremental Adjustment** is the form of completion in which a single term settles as slightly shifted from its prior position. Its formula is: X shifts slightly toward Y.

It must be Incremental Adjustment because Is leaves a state, Toward gathers the correction into one focal shift, and Correction supplies the change of standing. The correction is real, but minimal.

It is not Simple Placement, because there is a prior standing being altered. It is not Priority Rebalancing, because no retained plurality of weights remains. It is not Load Transfer, because no strain is moved into another term. It is not Limit Recalibration, because no boundary is reset. It is not Directional Pivot, because no ongoing trajectory is redirected.

Use Incremental Adjustment when completion requires a stabilized small shift. Reject it when the act merely places, reweights, transfers, resets a limit, or pivots direction.

## I29 — Correction · Toward · With → Priority Rebalancing

The seed of Priority Rebalancing is correction appearing as changed relative weight. More than one influence remains, but the balance among them must shift.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. Plurality remains structurally active. Orientation collapses to Toward, because the reweighting converges toward a focal adjustment. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the landing depends on changed priority, not mere balance.

**Priority Rebalancing** is the form of completion in which relative influence is reweighted around a convergent adjustment. Its formula is: X reweights relative influence toward Y.

It must be Priority Rebalancing because With retains multiple influences, Toward gathers them into focal adjustment, and Correction supplies the reweighting. The landing is not simply that forces balance; it is that the priority relation has been corrected.

It is not Incremental Adjustment, because plurality remains. It is not Load Transfer, because the correction is held as weighting rather than enacted movement of strain. It is not Mutual Counterbalance, because relation alone does not change priority. It is not Mutual Adjustment, because co-variation across a span is not dominant. It is not Mutual Constraint, because the terms have not integrated into reciprocal system restriction.

Use Priority Rebalancing when completion requires a changed weighting among retained influences. Reject it when the shift is singular, enacted as transfer, merely balanced, co-varied, or systemically constrained.

## I30 — Correction · Toward · Does → Load Transfer

The seed of Load Transfer is correction under strain. Something bears pressure, burden, weight, or structural demand that must move elsewhere.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. Transfer must happen. Orientation collapses to Toward, because the correction gathers into a focal destination for strain. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the action modifies a prior stressed standing.

**Load Transfer** is the form of completion in which structural strain is actively moved into another term. Its formula is: X transfers structural strain into Y.

It must be Load Transfer because Does requires enacted movement, Toward requires convergence toward a receiving term, and Correction supplies strain-relief as the dominant transformation.

It is not Priority Rebalancing, because transfer is enacted rather than held as weighting. It is not Incremental Adjustment, because the result is not a slight state-shift. It is not Directional Pivot, because the field is not retained as ongoing trajectory. It is not Role Integration, because no systemic role is being fulfilled. It is not Field Distribution, because the action is corrective transfer rather than expansive spread.

Use Load Transfer when an act corrects by moving strain, pressure, burden, or load into another term. Reject it when the act merely adjusts, reweights, redirects, fulfills a role, or distributes across a field.

## I31 — Correction · Across · Is → Oppositional Test

The seed of Oppositional Test is correction through countercase. A standing is not simply opposed; it is checked by opposition, inversion, or contrast.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Is. The landing is a tested standing, not a held counterpart resolution and not an enacted pivot. Orientation collapses to Across, because the test retains a span between the standing and its opposite. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the opposition functions to test or check what is already in play.

**Oppositional Test** is the form of completion in which a standing is tested through contrast or inversion across a retained field. Its formula is: X is tested by opposition to Y.

It must be Oppositional Test because Is leaves the result as a standing under test, Across retains the counter-span, and Correction supplies the checking transformation. The opposition is instrumental, not final.

It is not Polar Opposition, because the opposition is not the landing itself; it functions as a test. It is not Counterpart Resolution, because no held counterpart resolves the issue. It is not Directional Pivot, because no redirection is enacted. It is not Structural Restoration, because no repair has completed. It is not Necessary Implication, because the relation is not closure-level necessity.

Use Oppositional Test when completion requires a standing to be checked against an opposite or countercase. Reject it when opposition itself is the result, when a counterpart resolves, when a pivot occurs, when restoration completes, or when necessity binds.

## I32 — Correction · Across · With → Counterpart Resolution

The seed of Counterpart Resolution is correction through a retained other. Something cannot be corrected by a single shift or a mere test; a counterpart must remain present as the resolving structure.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. The counterpart remains structurally active. Orientation collapses to Across, because correction spans between the original standing and its counterpart. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the counterpart is not merely related; it resolves a mismatch or incompletion.

**Counterpart Resolution** is the form of completion in which correction resolves through a held counterpart relation. Its formula is: X is corrected through counterpart Y.

It must be Counterpart Resolution because With holds the counterpart, Across retains the corrective span, and Correction supplies the resolution. The counterpart is not decorative. It is the condition by which correction lands.

It is not Oppositional Test, because the counterpart resolves rather than merely tests. It is not Directional Pivot, because no enacted redirection occurs. It is not Formal Correspondence, because correction dominates relation. It is not Mutual Adjustment, because co-variation alone does not resolve the standing. It is not Identity Collapse, because the terms do not collapse into sameness.

Use Counterpart Resolution when correction requires a counterpart to remain structurally present. Reject it when the other term only tests, redirects, corresponds, co-varies, or collapses into identity.

## I33 — Correction · Across · Does → Directional Pivot

The seed of Directional Pivot is correction of trajectory. Something is already moving, oriented, or underway, but its direction must change.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. Pivoting must happen. Orientation collapses to Across, because the ongoing field remains active during the redirection. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the action changes direction rather than merely opening a new path.

**Directional Pivot** is the form of completion in which a term changes direction within an ongoing structure. Its formula is: X pivots direction toward Y.

It must be Directional Pivot because Does requires enacted redirection, Across retains the trajectory-field, and Correction supplies the change of course. The prior direction remains relevant as what is corrected.

It is not Mutual Adjustment, because co-variation is not enough. It is not Cyclic Recurrence, because there is no return through a cycle. It is not Directional Reversal, because the span remains retained rather than released into opposite direction. It is not Path Variation, because the dominant transformation is correction, not expanded routing. It is not Load Transfer, because strain is not moved into another term.

Use Directional Pivot when an ongoing trajectory is correctively redirected. Reject it when the act co-varies, recurs, reverses, varies by path, or transfers load.

## I34 — Correction · Away · Is → Limit Recalibration

The seed of Limit Recalibration is a boundary becoming inadequate. The limit is not newly imposed and not simply exceeded; it must be reset.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Is. The landing is the new standing of the limit. Orientation collapses to Away, because the previous boundary-setting falls away. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the limit is adjusted rather than cut, grown past, or held invariant.

**Limit Recalibration** is the form of completion in which a boundary is reset as the new standing. Its formula is: X resets its boundary as Y.

It must be Limit Recalibration because Is leaves a state, Away releases the prior limit, and Correction supplies recalibration. The boundary remains, but its measure has changed.

It is not Boundary Imposition, because the boundary is adjusted rather than first imposed. It is not Incremental Adjustment, because the correction concerns a limit, not a general small shift. It is not Edge Growth, because the edge is reset rather than exceeded through growth. It is not Structural Invariance, because change has occurred. It is not Complete Cessation, because the boundary is not ending continuation absolutely.

Use Limit Recalibration when completion resets what counts as the boundary. Reject it when the act imposes a boundary, shifts slightly, grows past an edge, remains invariant, or ends continuation.

---

## I35 — Correction · Away · With → Directional Reversal

The seed of Directional Reversal is correction through turnabout. The prior direction cannot be merely adjusted; it must be released and inverted.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to With. The before-and-after directions remain structurally present. Orientation collapses to Away, because the prior direction is released. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because reversal changes a prior course rather than merely opposing it.

**Directional Reversal** is the form of completion in which direction reverses while relational continuity remains. Its formula is: X reverses direction into Y.

It must be Directional Reversal because With holds the relation between prior and reversed direction, Away releases the original course, and Correction supplies the reversal.

It is not Directional Pivot, because Pivot retains the ongoing span while Reversal releases the prior direction. It is not Polar Opposition, because opposition is a state, not corrective reversal. It is not Continuous Diffusion, because the transition is not boundaryless opening. It is not Dynamic Equilibrium, because the motion does not remain in oscillating balance. It is not Complete Cessation, because movement continues in reversed form.

Use Directional Reversal when completion requires reversal rather than pivot, opposition, diffusion, equilibrium, or termination. Reject it when the direction merely shifts, stands opposed, transitions gradually, oscillates, or ends.

---

## I36 — Correction · Away · Does → Structural Restoration

The seed of Structural Restoration is disorder becoming correctable. Something has fallen from workable form, and the corrective pressure is toward repair.

Cultivation collapses terminal mode to Does. Restoration must happen. Orientation collapses to Away, because the disordered condition falls away. Operator dominance collapses to Correction, because the action repairs a prior structural failure.

**Structural Restoration** is the form of completion in which a structure is returned from disorder into restored form. Its formula is: X returns from disorder into Y.

It must be Structural Restoration because Does requires enacted repair, Away releases the broken or disordered state, and Correction supplies restoration. The result is not merely stable; it is repaired.

It is not Directional Reversal, because restoration is enacted repair rather than reversed direction. It is not Convergent Stabilization, because the operator is correction, not integration into stability. It is not Complete Cessation, because the result is restored structure, not termination. It is not Final Enactment, because the emphasis is repair rather than completed execution. It is not Functional Union, because the issue is restoration of structure, not formation of a unit.

Use Structural Restoration when an act completes by repairing or restoring a structure. Reject it when the act reverses, stabilizes, terminates, executes finally, or forms a new functional unity.
