# Book II — The Atlas of Remainders

## A Field Guide to the 54 Forms of Completion

This is the second book beneath **How Sentences Come to Rest**.

Where the first book establishes that sentences complete by coming to rest, this book develops the finite grammar of those rests. Its subject is the invariant: the stable remainder produced when an open field of possible completion contracts through one operator, one orientation, and one terminal mode.

The governing spine is:

**Operator · Orientation · Terminal Mode → Invariant**

This book is not a taxonomy of meanings. It is a field guide to landings. Each invariant chapter should cultivate the invariant from a seed, show why it must be produced by its coordinate, and define it exactly by what it is not.

## Chapter method

Each invariant chapter uses this structure:

1. Seed pressure — the first unstable pressure from which the invariant may grow.
2. Cultivation — the collapse of terminal mode, orientation, and operator dominance.
3. Coordinate declaration — the exact invariant coordinate.
4. Positive definition — what the invariant is.
5. Negative definition — what the invariant is not.
6. Rival exclusion — nearest cells and boundary conflicts.
7. Operational use — when to apply or reject the invariant.

The goal is not resemblance-based naming. The invariant must be computed from the surviving coordinate and verified by eliminating neighboring landings.
